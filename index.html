Section 2

Executive Summary

The strategic importance of digital communication in Irish grocery retail is increasing even though grocery remains predominantly store-led. Bord Bia estimated that online grocery represented 6.1% of take-home grocery sales in early 2026, with growth supported by improved digital interfaces, fulfillment capabilities and data-led loyalty rather than by temporary pandemic effects. By June, Worldpanel data showed online grocery sales increasing 16.8% year on year over the latest 12 weeks. At the same time, the market remained intensely competitive: in the period ending 14 June 2026, Dunnes held a 23.8% value share, Tesco 23.6%, SuperValu 19.5%, Lidl a record 15.0%, and Aldi 11.0%. Together these five therefore represented approximately 92.9% of the measured market. (Bord Bia, 2026; Worldpanel by Numerator, 2026).

The competitive environment also remained highly value-sensitive. Take-home grocery sales increased 4.8% in the four weeks to 14 June, while like-for-like grocery inflation was 4.77%. Promotional spending accounted for 23.5% of grocery value sales, its highest level of 2026 at that point. Branded products accounted for 49.1% of grocery sales over the latest 12 weeks, while own-label value sales rose 3.3% and pack volumes in premium ranges increased 10.3%. This is important strategically: Irish grocery communications in 2026 were operating in a market where price remained essential, but pure price communication was not sufficient to create distinctiveness. (Worldpanel by Numerator, 2026).

The empirical component of this study is a comparative cross-sectional digital-content analysis of 49 publicly accessible retailer-owned content units dated between 1 January and 30 June 2026. The coded corpus comprises Tesco (n=10), Lidl (n=10), Aldi (n=10), SuperValu (n=9) and Dunnes Stores (n=10). The sample concentrates on retailer-owned Facebook/video content, retailer websites and official campaign properties; official SuperValu TidyTowns channels are treated as part of SuperValu's owned communications architecture. Two local SuperValu franchise-page executions are retained because decentralised local ownership is structurally integral to the SuperValu model. The corpus is deliberately an analytical sample rather than a claim to represent every post published by every retailer.

Across the audited corpus, video accounted for 85.7% of content units. Value/promotion and community/Irish-provenance themes each represented 24.5% of the total sample, seasonal/cultural content 18.4%, food/product inspiration 14.3%, loyalty/convenience 12.2%, and purpose/partnership content 6.1%. The prevalence of video supports the wider literature suggesting that digital branded content increasingly competes not simply as advertising but as platform-native entertainment, information and social interaction. Creative variation and experiential relevance are therefore material to consumer response rather than being cosmetic executional choices. (Ashley and Tuten, 2015; Voorveld et al., 2018).

The most important finding is that the five retailers are not pursuing the same digital strategy despite competing in essentially the same grocery market.

Tesco emerged as the strongest full-funnel digital ecosystem. Its owned communications combined Clubcard incentives, online and rapid-shopping convenience, seasonal product content, premium food, supplier provenance and community participation. Tesco therefore demonstrated the clearest link between content and identifiable customer-journey infrastructure: content could lead to Clubcard activation, online shopping, rapid delivery, click-and-collect or community participation. Examples included Double Clubcard Points communication, same-day Click & Collect messaging, 45-minute delivery content, supplier storytelling and Street Feast participation.

Lidl displayed the strongest combination of social-native communication, value messaging and brand-purpose activation. Its "Greatness Deserves to be Seen" LGFA campaign went substantially beyond conventional sponsorship: Lidl committed regular advertising inventory to women's Gaelic football and connected the campaign to research about media visibility and gender inequality in Irish sport. At the other end of the funnel, Lidl Plus savings, Super Savers and "More to Value" content repeatedly translated the brand into immediate economic utility. Lidl therefore operated a dual platform of cultural relevance plus demonstrable value. (Lidl Ireland, 2026a).

Aldi demonstrated the highest strategic message consistency. Its 2026 platform, "ALDI. It's Not Complicated.", allowed numerous retail propositions—weekly-shopping value, low prices, seasonal communications, quality Irish food and Specialbuys—to sit beneath one coherent organizing thought. Industry reporting confirms that the platform was deployed across digital, radio and out-of-home media. The strength of Aldi's model is therefore less the breadth of messages than its ability to make different messages reinforce the same mental positioning: simplicity, low friction and dependable value. (Public Relations Institute of Ireland, 2026; Checkout, 2026).

SuperValu showed the strongest community-legitimacy platform but the weakest conversion orientation within the audited owned-content sample. SuperValu's TidyTowns infrastructure provides a particularly defensible brand asset: the 2026 competition launch continued a sponsorship relationship then in its 36th year, supported a nationwide network of almost 1,000 active TidyTowns groups, and complemented the retailer's structural position as a network of locally owned stores. The "Real Nourishment" GAA platform added sport, food and wellbeing. However, 67% of the coded SuperValu sample centered on community/Irish-provenance themes and only 22% was coded as explicitly promotional. This creates a strong reputation and belonging platform, but also implies an opportunity to connect community authority more directly to shopping, CRM and digital conversion. (Department of Rural and Community Development and the Gaeltacht, 2026; Sport for Business, 2026).

Dunnes Stores occupied the most distinctive premium-value hybrid position. Its grocery content combined explicit offers and voucher mechanics with Irish provenance, food inspiration and the premium Simply Better architecture. Content around Valentine's meals, Easter promotions, Irish produce and Neven Maguire reinforces a proposition in which savings do not require the brand to adopt a discount-store identity. Bord Bia similarly identifies Simply Better, Irish artisan suppliers, chef-led storytelling and money-off vouchers as central to Dunnes' food proposition. (Bord Bia, 2026).

The promotional-intensity measure reinforces these distinctions. In the coded sample, Lidl and Aldi each recorded 50% explicitly promotional content, Dunnes 40%, Tesco 30% and SuperValu 22%. These percentages should not be interpreted as estimates of each retailer's total social feed; they describe the audited corpus. Nevertheless, the pattern is strategically coherent with the underlying propositions: discounters repeatedly dramatize price, Dunnes combines promotional triggers with quality cues, Tesco distributes content across multiple journey stages, and SuperValu allocates more visible communication space to community assets.

Observed public Facebook video views also illustrate why simplistic "engagement league tables" are inappropriate. Among sampled videos for which an indexed public view count was visible, Aldi had a median of approximately 7,800 views (n=5) but included a 1.1 million-view Specialbuys outlier; Lidl's median was approximately 6,750 (n=6), Tesco's approximately 2,500 (n=5), and Dunnes' approximately 2,650 (n=2). A Tesco Mini Eggs execution displayed approximately 262,000 views, Lidl's Kickstart and Easter-hosting executions approximately 71,000 and 82,000 respectively, and Aldi's Specialbuys execution approximately 1.1 million. These figures demonstrate the potential of culturally or product-relevant video, but not relative account effectiveness because follower bases, paid amplification, time online and platform distribution were not controlled.

From a management perspective, four implications dominate.

First, value communication has become a hygiene factor rather than a durable positioning on its own. Each retailer must communicate value, but sustainable differentiation comes from how that value is framed: Tesco through personalization and convenience; Lidl through energetic challenger behavior; Aldi through simplicity; SuperValu through local community legitimacy; and Dunnes through premium quality combined with savings.

Second, owned content should be managed as customer-journey infrastructure rather than as a publishing calendar. The largest strategic opportunity is to connect Reach content to identifiable Act, Convert and Engage outcomes through apps, loyalty accounts, shoppable recipes, store missions and retargetable first-party audiences. This reflects customer-journey research which argues that experience is created through sequences of interconnected touchpoints rather than isolated communications. (Lemon and Verhoef, 2016).

Third, distinctive brand platforms outperform disconnected campaigns strategically, even where individual posts perform well tactically. Aldi provides the clearest 2026 example through "It's Not Complicated"; Lidl provides an effective master narrative around value and cultural participation; SuperValu owns powerful community properties; Dunnes has Simply Better; Tesco's opportunity is to integrate its many strong functional assets under a more immediately recognizable Irish brand narrative.

Finally, performance management should move away from likes or raw views toward a RACE-based scorecard measuring qualified reach, interaction, conversion and repeat engagement. RACE's Reach–Act–Convert–Engage logic is particularly applicable to grocery because the category generates frequent transactions and therefore creates opportunities to connect communications directly with loyalty, online baskets, voucher redemption and repeat purchase. (Chaffey, 2025).

Section 3

Introduction and Literature Review

Industry context

Irish grocery retail in 2026 is structurally concentrated but strategically dynamic. Bord Bia characterizes the sector as a mature, highly competitive market in which five major supermarket groups dominate share and in which full-service supermarkets face continuing pressure from value-focused discounters. In March 2026, Dunnes held 24.3%, Tesco 23.6%, SuperValu 19.3%, Lidl 14.3% and Aldi 11.1%; by the Worldpanel period ending 14 June, Lidl had reached a record 15.0%, Dunnes stood at 23.8%, Tesco at 23.6%, SuperValu at 19.5% and Aldi at 11.0%. (Bord Bia, 2026; Worldpanel by Numerator, 2026).

This is not a market in which communications can be separated from economic conditions. In March, Bord Bia, citing CSO figures, reported food and non-alcoholic beverage inflation of 2.3% year on year; later Worldpanel data placed like-for-like grocery inflation at 4.77% in the four weeks ending 14 June. Promotional expenditure had risen to 23.5% of grocery value sales by June. The same June data showed average consumers visiting supermarkets 23.1 times over four weeks, one trip more than in the previous month. Grocery remains high frequency, and relatively small changes in perceived value, convenience or loyalty therefore have potentially repeated behavioral consequences. (Bord Bia, 2026; Worldpanel by Numerator, 2026).

Broader retail data provide useful context. CSO figures for June 2026 show total retail-sales volume rising 0.4% month on month and 0.6% year on year, while retail-sales value increased 2.8% annually. The divergence between modest volume expansion and faster value growth illustrates why retailers cannot interpret nominal sales growth as evidence that customers have become insensitive to price. (CSO, 2026).

Digital is increasingly embedded in this competitive environment. Bord Bia estimated online grocery at 6.1% of total take-home grocery sales in early 2026 and highlighted retailer investment in apps, rapid fulfillment, digital loyalty and online interfaces. Its market review specifically identifies Tesco's Whoosh proposition and SuperValu's rapid-delivery partnership as evidence that online grocery is expanding beyond large scheduled baskets toward "top-up" and urgent missions. (Bord Bia, 2026).

Worldpanel data later in the study period strengthen the case. Online grocery sales were up 16.8% year on year during the latest 12 weeks to June, with new online customers contributing more than €26 million in additional sales. Digital grocery is therefore economically smaller than stores but strategically disproportionate: it influences convenience, customer data, loyalty, search, promotion and the relationship between communication and transaction. (Worldpanel by Numerator, 2026).

Research problem and question

The managerial problem addressed in this report is that grocery retailers face an apparent contradiction. They must communicate price and promotion frequently enough to remain credible in a value-sensitive environment, yet excessive reliance on discount messages can reduce brand distinctiveness and train audiences to view communications only through a transactional lens.

Digital content gives retailers an opportunity to resolve this tension because the same ecosystem can deliver product discovery, entertainment, recipes, community stories, promotional offers, loyalty benefits, fulfillment propositions and post-purchase engagement. The strategic issue is therefore not simply whether retailers use digital media, but what role their content assigns to the brand at different points of the customer relationship.

The primary research question is:

How did Tesco, Lidl, Aldi, SuperValu and Dunnes Stores use retailer-owned digital content between January and June 2026 to communicate value, differentiate their brands, influence the grocery customer journey and build consumer engagement in Ireland?

The analysis addresses four supporting questions: what content pillars and formats dominate each retailer's audited output; what proportion of sampled communications primarily seeks Reach, Act, Convert or Engage outcomes; how intensively does each retailer use explicit promotional messaging; and how coherently do observed digital communications reinforce each brand's competitive positioning?

Digital content and consumer engagement

Academic research indicates that branded social content should not be understood simply as an online equivalent of traditional advertising. Ashley and Tuten's research on branded social content identifies substantial variation in creative strategy and suggests that the type of content deployed matters for consumer engagement. Social environments reward communications that create reasons to interact rather than simply reproducing one-directional promotional messages. (Ashley and Tuten, 2015).

Hollebeek, Glynn and Brodie conceptualize consumer-brand engagement in social media as involving cognitive, emotional and behavioral dimensions. This distinction matters in grocery because a content item can have value even where it does not create an immediate purchase. A community story may strengthen identification; an entertaining seasonal video may improve salience; a recipe may stimulate consideration; a loyalty offer may trigger a transaction. Treating all four as equivalent "posts" loses strategically relevant information. (Hollebeek, Glynn and Brodie, 2014).

Platform context also affects response. Voorveld et al. find that engagement with social media and advertising varies by platform experience, reinforcing the argument that managers should avoid mechanically transporting one creative asset across channels. Content should be designed for the behavior, format and motivations associated with the platform in which it appears. (Voorveld et al., 2018).

This has direct relevance to the present findings. An 85.7% video share in the audited corpus does not mean that video is automatically superior. Rather, video has become the dominant executional language through which retailers attempt to earn attention. The managerial question shifts from "should we use video?" toward "what distinctive job is each video performing?"

Integrated Marketing Communications

Integrated Marketing Communications provides a useful framework for testing whether multiple executions reinforce a coherent strategic idea. Kliatchko's revised conceptualization of IMC emphasizes audience-centered integration and coordinated communications rather than treating different communications tools as autonomous activities. (Kliatchko, 2008).

For grocery retailers, IMC can be assessed at several levels. At the basic level, visual identity and promotional claims should be consistent. At a stronger level, social content, loyalty communications, websites, store messaging and campaigns should direct consumers toward the same proposition. At the strongest level, paid, shared, owned and earned activity should contribute to one recognizable brand meaning.

The distinction becomes important when comparing Aldi and Tesco. Aldi's narrower proposition enables exceptionally clear message integration: "The Weekly Shop. It's Not Complicated", "No schemes. Just low prices. It's Not Complicated" and "Summer. It's Not Complicated" are variations of the same strategic narrative. Tesco, by contrast, has a broader ecosystem—Clubcard, rapid delivery, premium Tesco Finest content, suppliers and community partnerships—which creates more customer-journey functionality but also creates a greater integration challenge.

Customer journey and RACE

Lemon and Verhoef argue that customer experience develops across a journey involving multiple touchpoints, including those controlled by the firm and those outside its direct control. This provides a more strategically useful approach to grocery content than assessing every item using the same engagement KPI. (Lemon and Verhoef, 2016).

The RACE planning model operationalizes a related concept through Reach, Act, Convert and Engage. Reach creates awareness and traffic; Act encourages interaction or progression; Convert produces an economically meaningful action; and Engage develops longer-term relationships and repeat activity. (Chaffey, 2025).

In this report, RACE is not treated as an academic causal model. It is used as a managerial classification framework. A Mini Eggs entertainment video can predominantly perform Reach; a recipe or application invitation can perform Act; a product offer or online-delivery call-to-action can perform Convert; and a Clubcard relationship incentive can perform Engage. This forces the analysis to distinguish communication activity from intended business outcome.

PESO and channel architecture

The PESO framework separates communications into Paid, Earned, Shared and Owned media and was popularized by Gini Dietrich as a practical integrated-communications model. (Dietrich, 2014).

The coded dataset in this study deliberately concentrates on owned content, because the objective is to compare what the retailers themselves chose to publish or host. PESO is then used interpretively to determine how those owned properties connect to wider communications.

Lidl's LGFA activation provides a good example. Its corporate release describes "Greatness Deserves to be Seen" as a fully integrated marketing and digital campaign, while the underlying sponsorship naturally generates shared conversation and earned sports/media coverage. Aldi's "It's Not Complicated" platform similarly extended across digital, radio and outdoor channels. These are strategically stronger than isolated social posts because multiple media types reinforce the same brand idea. (Lidl Ireland, 2026a; Public Relations Institute of Ireland, 2026).

Brand positioning

Brand-positioning analysis asks what distinctive association a retailer is seeking to occupy relative to competitors. In grocery, functional points of parity are extensive: all five retailers need acceptable range, product quality, price competitiveness and convenient access. Sustainable differentiation therefore depends on the meaning attached to those fundamentals.

Using Keller's brand-management logic, this study interprets positioning through recurring associations rather than slogans alone: value, simplicity, convenience, Irishness, community, quality, entertainment, expertise, purpose and premium food. (Keller, 2013).

This approach produces a more commercially meaningful comparison than asking which retailer posts most often. Aldi's differentiation is simplified value; Lidl's is energetic challenger value with cultural relevance; SuperValu's is local belonging and community legitimacy; Dunnes is quality-led Irish premium value; and Tesco is personalized value plus breadth and omnichannel utility. These conclusions are developed empirically below rather than assumed from stated brand propositions.

Section 4

Methodology and Dataset

Research design

The study adopts a comparative cross-sectional digital-content analysis, bounded to retailer-owned content published from 1 January through 30 June 2026 inclusive.

"Cross-sectional" is used here in the managerial-research sense of a bounded comparative snapshot across five organizations rather than as a claim that all observations occurred on one calendar date. The six-month window captures recurring retail occasions including New Year value messaging, Valentine's Day, Pancake Tuesday, Easter, St Patrick's Day, spring/summer activity, sports partnerships and community programs.

The design combines quantitative content coding with qualitative strategic interpretation. Quantitative analysis identifies visible differences in pillar mix, format, intended objective and promotional intensity. Qualitative analysis then asks how those differences contribute to brand positioning and integrated communications.

Unit of analysis

The unit of analysis is one unique retailer-owned digital communication artifact. An artifact may be a Facebook video/reel, an official brand post, a retailer webpage, corporate campaign page or official campaign-subbrand communication.

Paid advertising reported by trade publications and earned-media stories were not coded as owned-content units. They were used only to triangulate the scope, integration or strategic intent of campaigns. This distinction prevents paid-media intensity from artificially inflating a retailer's owned-content score.

For SuperValu, official SuperValu TidyTowns properties were included because the sponsorship is branded and operated as a continuing SuperValu communications platform. Two local franchise-page value executions were also retained and explicitly identified as such. This reflects SuperValu's organizational model: unlike the centrally controlled estates of Aldi or Lidl, SuperValu's consumer presence incorporates independently owned local retailers. Bord Bia notes that SuperValu operates predominantly through such an independently owned store model. (Bord Bia, 2026).

Sampling rules

Content qualified for inclusion when it met the following conditions simultaneously: it was published between January and June 2026; it was attributable to the retailer or an official retailer campaign property; it concerned grocery, FMCG, food, loyalty, grocery fulfillment, brand/community activity or a directly relevant retail occasion; and sufficient information was publicly accessible to classify the communication.

Content was excluded where it was primarily recruitment communication, mandatory recall information, opening-hour administration, duplicate publication of an identical creative, or—in the case of Dunnes—pure fashion/homeware material without a meaningful connection to grocery strategy.

The sampling strategy is stratified purposive rather than probabilistic. It prioritizes strategically interpretable material across promotions, products, convenience, community, seasonal occasions and partnerships. Consequently, percentages reported below should be read as the composition of the audited analytical corpus, not a statistical estimate of the complete six-month publishing universe.

This choice is important. Social platforms do not expose historic posts consistently to public web indexing, and some platforms provide substantially less historic metadata than others. Claiming exhaustive feed coverage would therefore create false methodological precision.

Validity and analytical caution

The coding framework creates consistent comparison, but it does not eliminate judgement. A single analyst conducted the coding; therefore, no inter-coder reliability statistic such as Cohen's kappa is reported. That is preferable to inventing reliability precision that the design cannot support.

Engagement is treated even more cautiously. Public views were captured only where discoverable, and no cross-retailer engagement rate is calculated because comparable impressions, organic reach, paid amplification, follower exposure and watch-time denominators are not publicly available. The study therefore uses views diagnostically, not as a definitive performance ranking.

This distinction is consistent with engagement research: observable behaviors such as viewing or interacting capture only part of a broader cognitive, emotional and behavioral relationship with a brand. (Hollebeek, Glynn and Brodie, 2014).

Section 5

Quantitative Results

Competitive market benchmark

The latest Worldpanel data inside the study period show an unusually tight contest at the top of Irish grocery. Dunnes led Tesco by only 0.2 percentage points, while Lidl reached a record 15.0%. SuperValu remained materially larger than either individual discounter, while Aldi held 11.0%. (Worldpanel by Numerator, 2026).

The strategic implication is not that social content caused these shares. No causal relationship can be established from the dataset. The relevance is that digital strategy is being deployed in a category where relatively small gains or losses in consumer preference can alter retailer rankings.

The movement from Bord Bia's March benchmark to the June Worldpanel period is particularly notable for Lidl. Bord Bia recorded Lidl at 14.3% in March; by June it reached 15.0%. Aldi was broadly stable around 11%, while the two market leaders remained separated by fractions of a percentage point. (Bord Bia, 2026; Worldpanel by Numerator, 2026).

The retailer profiles are visibly different.

Tesco had the largest proportion of loyalty/convenience content in the sample, reflecting Clubcard, Click & Collect and rapid-delivery communications. This fits Bord Bia's broader assessment that Tesco is investing in digital, unified commerce, personalization and rapid fulfillment. (Bord Bia, 2026).

Lidl's 40% value/promotion share reflects repeated Lidl Plus, Super Savers and "More to Value" communication, but its 20% purpose/partnership share distinguishes it from a purely transactional discount strategy. Its LGFA platform is the strongest example.

Aldi's sample combines product discovery and seasonal/cultural communication with value. This reflects the role of Specialbuys and the flexibility of the "It's Not Complicated" platform. A single positioning idea is able to support low-price, weekly-shop and seasonal messages without requiring separate campaign identities.

SuperValu's profile is exceptional: approximately two-thirds of audited units centered on community and Irish-provenance themes. This is heavily influenced by TidyTowns, but that influence is itself strategically meaningful. TidyTowns is not peripheral corporate philanthropy; it is a long-term branded property capable of giving SuperValu a credible role in communities outside the transaction.

Dunnes is more balanced. Promotional messages, food inspiration, Irish provenance and convenience all appear materially in the sample. This supports the interpretation that Dunnes is deliberately balancing a high-impact value mechanic with quality and premium food rather than optimizing communications around a single discount identity. Bord Bia identifies the same combination of Simply Better, Irish suppliers, chef-led content and voucher-driven value in its 2026 market assessment. (Bord Bia, 2026).

The most striking aggregate finding is the 42.9% Convert share. Grocery is a transactionally dense category and its retailers use digital content accordingly: offers, product availability, delivery and shopping propositions are frequently designed to move audiences toward an immediate commercial action.

Dunnes' 70% Convert classification is the highest in this corpus. The explanation is not simply that Dunnes discounts more. Its sampled communications repeatedly connect food content to identifiable shopping missions: meal deals, Easter eggs, turkey-and-ham offers, own-brand launches, same-day delivery and Click & Collect.

Lidl similarly exhibits a strong conversion orientation, but with a greater Act component because content such as Kickstart, entertainment and hosting sits alongside Lidl Plus and value messages.

SuperValu over-indexes on Act. TidyTowns content regularly asks communities, schools or committees to participate, enter or get involved rather than immediately to purchase. The communication is behavioral, but its desired behavior is civic participation.

Tesco is the only retailer in the coded sample with a meaningful Engage classification, driven by Clubcard relationship content. This should not be interpreted to mean the other retailers have no retention or loyalty activity; rather, Tesco's sampled owned communications more clearly surfaced persistent customer-account behavior as a content objective.

This difference matters because the strategic value of loyalty infrastructure extends beyond an individual promotional redemption. It creates identifiable first-party customer relationships and therefore the possibility of more personalized communication over time.

The 50% result for Lidl and Aldi is consistent with discounter economics but does not mean their digital strategies are interchangeable. Lidl's explicit savings communications coexist with sports, supplier-development and entertainment content. Aldi embeds many value claims inside a broader simplification strategy. The distinction is strategically important: promotional intensity measures how often price is communicated; positioning measures what the price communication means for the brand.

Dunnes is particularly interesting because its 40% promotional intensity is not expressed through a discount-retailer aesthetic alone. Valentine's dinner, Easter food and voucher mechanics are often paired with premium or occasion-based cues.

SuperValu's comparatively low sampled promotional intensity does not imply weak price competition. Its "Still Super, Now Better Value" campaign communicated more than 500 price reductions in early 2026. Rather, the lower rate in the audited owned-content sample reflects the amount of digital space given to community programs. (Adworld, 2026).

This does not prove that consumers "do not care" about Irish suppliers. It suggests that light, seasonal, recognizable product content can obtain substantially greater platform reach than a more corporate supplier narrative under certain conditions. Creative treatment and distribution therefore matter as much as strategic theme.

Lidl's Kickstart supplier-development post displayed approximately 71,000 views, while an Easter-hosting execution displayed approximately 82,000. This is strategically encouraging because it demonstrates that both supplier/community content and occasion-based inspiration can achieve material visibility when packaged in accessible social video.

The managerial conclusion is not "maximize views". Raw video views cannot distinguish passive autoplay exposure from high-quality customer response, nor can they reveal incremental sales. A useful grocery-content measurement model must connect platform attention to downstream customer behavior.

Section 6

Qualitative and Framework Analysis

Tesco

The strongest digital customer-journey architecture

Tesco's principal advantage in the 2026 sample is not one exceptional campaign. It is the number of strategically useful digital pathways available to the customer.

The retailer can communicate a food product, then convert through online grocery; communicate convenience, then move customers into same-day collection or rapid delivery; communicate price, then attach it to Clubcard; and communicate community participation through a physical-store network. Bord Bia similarly describes Tesco Ireland as operating a broad multi-format estate supported by major online-grocery capability, Clubcard data and increasing personalization. (Bord Bia, 2026).

February Double Clubcard Points content illustrates the distinction between temporary price promotion and relationship infrastructure. The immediate message is an incentive, but the consumer must participate through an identifiable loyalty account. The resulting communication therefore operates both as a short-term sales stimulus and as an engagement mechanism.

Convenience is the second strong pillar. Same-day Click & Collect communication and "shopping delivered in 45 minutes" reduce the perceived time cost of grocery shopping. This is strategically important because convenience competes with price as a form of consumer value. A shopper who can resolve an urgent mission quickly may judge value through saved time as well as the shelf price.

Tesco also avoids reducing the brand to utility. Tesco Finest Irish Angus food content and Killowen supplier storytelling introduce quality and provenance cues, while Street Feast allows the retailer to participate in neighborhood belonging.

The Street Feast relationship is particularly important from a PESO perspective. Tesco-owned communications can promote the initiative, but the actual event is enacted by communities and consequently creates shared and earned content outside Tesco's own channels. This is more valuable reputationally than a corporate claim that Tesco is "community focused" because consumers participate in the evidence themselves.

Tesco's weakness is the reverse of its strength: breadth can dilute narrative simplicity. Clubcard, Finest, Whoosh, Click & Collect, suppliers, community and seasonal entertainment all make sense individually, but they do not automatically create one sharp memory structure.

From an IMC perspective, Tesco therefore has strong functional integration but less obvious single-idea integration than Aldi. Tesco's strategic opportunity is to make its ecosystem feel like one promise—for example, personalized help that makes everyday Irish life easier—rather than a portfolio of capabilities.

Lidl

Challenger energy, cultural participation and tangible value

Lidl's 2026 communication is strategically notable because it avoids choosing between discount credibility and brand meaning.

"Greatness Deserves to be Seen" is the clearest example. Lidl launched the 2026 National Football League campaign with Katie Taylor while extending its LGFA partnership through 2030 and announcing a further €7.5 million investment. Its own research found strong public appetite for women's sport but continued perceived inequality in media representation; Lidl responded by allocating its regular television advertising space to LGFA moments rather than merely placing its logo beside athletes. (Lidl Ireland, 2026a).

This is strategically sophisticated because the communications mechanism expresses the social issue. The problem identified is under-visibility; the creative response literally gives women's sport advertising visibility.

The content therefore satisfies several IMC requirements simultaneously. Sponsorship strategy, consumer research, video creative, digital distribution and media investment all point toward the same meaning. The campaign is not a CSR statement attached to a retail brand; it is a communications behavior that demonstrates the position.

Lidl's purpose content is then balanced by direct economic proof. "Save up to 30% with Lidl Plus", Super Savers, "Big shop, BIGGER savings" and "Same groceries, BIGGER savings" make affordability highly legible.

This balance is a major strength. In a high-inflation environment, purpose without value risks feeling disconnected from household concerns; value without brand meaning creates commoditization. Lidl attempts to hold both.

Kickstart provides a third pillar. A February video celebrated ten years of the Irish supplier-development program and introduced the 2026 participants; the indexed result displayed approximately 71,000 views. This enables Lidl to address a potential weakness of an international discounter—the perception that low prices might imply weak local embeddedness—through supplier stories and Irish entrepreneurship.

Lidl also shows stronger willingness than most traditional grocery brands to behave like a social-media participant. April Fools content, seasonal hosting and reaction-oriented value videos create a less corporate voice.

From a RACE perspective, however, the sampled content is concentrated in Reach, Act and Convert, with less visible persistent Engage activity than Tesco. Lidl Plus is an obvious mechanism capable of closing this gap; the strategic opportunity is to turn more high-reach cultural content into an identifiable Lidl Plus relationship without making purpose communications feel transactional.

Aldi

The clearest master-brand discipline

Aldi's "It's Not Complicated" is arguably the most strategically disciplined platform observed in the study.

The campaign launched nationally in January 2026 with a simplicity-centered brand proposition, and industry sources reported its extension across radio, outdoor and digital channels. (Public Relations Institute of Ireland, 2026; Checkout, 2026).

The platform works because it is sufficiently broad to organize communications without becoming meaningless. "The Weekly Shop. It's Not Complicated", "No schemes. Just low prices. It's Not Complicated", "Quality Irish Food. It's Not Complicated" and "Summer. It's Not Complicated" translate different retail tasks into the same cognitive benefit: shopping at Aldi reduces complexity.

This is a stronger positioning mechanism than repeatedly stating "great value". Every grocery retailer claims value. Aldi attempts to redefine value as the absence of complexity, schemes and unnecessary friction.

The approach also creates a useful contrast with Tesco. Tesco's loyalty architecture makes personalization a source of value; Aldi's messaging can make the absence of complex loyalty mechanics part of the proposition. Neither approach is universally superior. They target different definitions of shopping convenience.

Specialbuys provide a separate but compatible content engine. Their role is discovery, novelty and scarcity. One March 2026 Aldi Specialbuys video surfaced with approximately 1.1 million public views, the largest indexed count in the audited engagement subset.

That result illustrates the strategic role of non-grocery content around the grocery mission. The Aisle of Aldi encourages store visitation through discovery and unpredictability, which can increase mental salience even where the featured product is not an FMCG item. In a strict grocery-content study, such material must be controlled analytically; as a retail strategy, however, it is part of Aldi's traffic-generation system.

The GROW with ALDI supplier program adds local provenance. A February owned video introducing 2026 winners displayed approximately 39,000 indexed views. This gives Aldi an Irish-supplier narrative without requiring the retailer to abandon its simplified-value positioning.

Aldi's largest strategic opportunity lies in Engage. Simplicity is strong for acquisition and conversion, but digital growth increasingly rewards identifiable customer relationships. The management challenge is to develop personalization or digital utility without contradicting "No schemes. Just low prices."

That suggests a different model from Tesco: useful digital tools that reduce shopping effort rather than a heavily gamified loyalty proposition.

SuperValu

Community legitimacy as a strategic moat

SuperValu is the most structurally different competitor in the analysis. Its independent-retailer network gives the brand a local presence that is difficult for a centralized chain to replicate credibly. Bord Bia describes SuperValu's position as Ireland's local retailer and highlights fresh food, Irish sourcing and community focus as central to its proposition. (Bord Bia, 2026).

TidyTowns is the strongest expression of this position. The 2026 competition was officially launched on 23 March, with SuperValu then in its 36th year as sponsor. The competition now involves almost 1,000 active TidyTowns groups, and 2025 saw a record 929 entries. (Department of Rural and Community Development and the Gaeltacht, 2026; SuperValu, 2026a).

The value of TidyTowns is not simply reach. It creates behavioral evidence of positioning. A retailer claiming to support local communities can be challenged as advertising. A retailer associated for decades with volunteers physically improving local towns possesses a more difficult-to-copy brand association.

The official digital communication around the program reinforces participation: application deadlines, schools initiatives and inclusive-community awards give the campaign repeated reasons to communicate beyond a single sponsorship announcement.

SuperValu's "Real Nourishment" GAA campaign broadens this community platform toward food and wellbeing. The campaign linked the retailer's football sponsorship with nutritional choices and featured inter-county athletes, allowing SuperValu to connect its food expertise with an existing Irish cultural property. (Sport for Business, 2026).

The early-2026 "Still Super, Now Better Value" campaign demonstrates that SuperValu also recognized the need to strengthen price perception, promoting more than 500 reductions across grocery and household products. (Adworld, 2026).

The strategic issue is therefore not a lack of value messaging. It is integration between value, local community and digital conversion.

In the audited sample, TidyTowns communications often terminate at participation. That is appropriate for campaign credibility, but a broader digital strategy could enable consumers who engage with community content to encounter locally relevant recipes, supplier stories, Real Rewards benefits or store-specific offers without turning TidyTowns itself into a sales promotion.

SuperValu's decentralized architecture creates both an advantage and a challenge. Local store pages can communicate with high relevance, but decentralization increases the risk of inconsistent tone, creative quality, offer presentation and measurement. A federated content operating model is therefore more appropriate than purely centralized control.

Dunnes Stores

Premiumization without surrendering value

Dunnes occupies a strategically valuable middle ground. It can behave as a price-competitive grocer while also holding premium associations through food halls, Simply Better, Irish artisan suppliers and chef partnerships.

Bord Bia identifies a tiered private-label architecture running from Everyday Savers through core Dunnes products to Simply Better and notes that more than 90 Irish producers contribute to Simply Better. It also highlights Neven Maguire content and Dunnes' money-off voucher mechanics as central elements of the retailer's consumer proposition. (Bord Bia, 2026).

The 2026 owned-content sample reflects this duality. A Valentine's Simply Better meal deal for €20 converts premium occasion cues into an accessible basket proposition. Easter communications use both direct price mechanics—half-price eggs and multibuy offers—and food inspiration based on Irish produce.

St Patrick's content inviting consumers to shop Irish favorites reinforces national provenance, while Neven Maguire/Simply Better activity introduces culinary authority.

Dunnes also increasingly connects content to fulfillment. Same-day delivery and free Click & Collect communications reduce friction and signal that the retailer's digital proposition is not simply a brand showcase.

The strategic opportunity is to make these elements feel like one digital food ecosystem. Simply Better currently performs strongly as a product and quality architecture; vouchers perform strongly as a value mechanism; delivery performs convenience. The next step is to connect them through content journeys.

For example, a Neven Maguire recipe should not end as inspiration. It can generate a saved recipe, automatically populated online basket, identified VALUEclub interaction, voucher trigger and subsequent personalized recommendation. That is the difference between content marketing and content-enabled commerce.

Section 7

Strategic Implications and Managerial Recommendations

The central strategic conclusion is that digital content should be managed as a commercial and brand system, not as a social-media output function.

The retailers already generate substantial volumes of competent communications. The next source of competitive advantage lies less in increasing posting frequency and more in improving distinctiveness, journey integration, first-party data capture, local relevance and measurement discipline.

Move from content calendars to customer-journey portfolios

Most content teams are organized around publication: what is going live Monday, what campaign starts Thursday, what seasonal post is needed next week. That operating model encourages output without forcing the business to identify what customer behavior should change.

Every major content brief should instead be assigned a primary RACE role.

A Reach asset should have a defined audience and incremental-reach target.

An Act asset should generate a meaningful interaction: recipe save, product exploration, competition entry, store search or app visit.

A Convert asset should connect to an attributable basket, voucher, ecommerce session or store action.

An Engage asset should increase identified repeat behavior, loyalty activity, preference capture or advocacy.

This recommendation follows directly from the customer-journey literature and the RACE lifecycle approach. (Lemon and Verhoef, 2016; Chaffey, 2025).

A proposed grocery-content scorecard is:

RACE stage Weak KPI Recommended management KPI
Reach Total video views Incremental qualified reach; completed views; new audience penetration
Act Likes Product-page visits; recipe saves; store searches; app opens; engaged sessions
Convert Clicks Attributed baskets; voucher redemptions; digital orders; incremental sales
Engage Followers Repeat purchase; active loyalty members; retention; frequency; personalized-content response

Protect distinctiveness as price competition increases

The June market data show high promotional participation and ongoing grocery inflation. In such conditions, managers can easily overcorrect by dedicating increasing proportions of communication to price. (Worldpanel by Numerator, 2026).

That response can generate short-term traffic while damaging long-term distinctiveness.

The recommended principle is:

Every retailer must prove value; no retailer should attempt to make generic value its entire identity.

Aldi should continue framing value as uncomplicated.

Lidl should frame it as intelligent challenger behavior and "more to value."

Tesco should increasingly frame it through personalized savings plus convenience.

Dunnes should combine savings with food quality and premium accessibility.

SuperValu should present value as compatible with local quality, service and community.

Build recognizable content franchises

Algorithms favor continual novelty, but brands benefit from repeated structures. The solution is not repeated identical advertising; it is recognizable content franchises.

For Tesco, an owned franchise could demonstrate how real Irish households use Clubcard, online services and Tesco products to solve everyday shopping problems.

For Lidl, repeatable cultural formats can sit beside "More to Value", while LGFA and Kickstart remain defensible long-term pillars.

For Aldi, "It's Not Complicated" already provides the master structure; recurring short-form series should dramatize specific consumer frustrations and their simple solution.

For SuperValu, local-store storytelling should be standardized enough to be recognizable while retaining genuinely local people, suppliers and events.

For Dunnes, Simply Better can become a much larger creator-led food-content system centered on chefs, producers, recipes and occasions.

The objective is mental availability through consistent structures without creative fatigue.

Connect high-reach video to first-party relationships

The engagement examples demonstrate that grocery content can occasionally achieve very large platform distribution: Aldi's observed Specialbuys outlier exceeded one million displayed views; Tesco's Mini Eggs execution exceeded 250,000; Lidl's selected Kickstart and Easter content exceeded 70,000.

The missed opportunity occurs when that attention ends inside the social platform.

High-reach content should create optional, low-friction movement into retailer-controlled environments: saved recipes, shopping lists, loyalty/app content, store locators, product alerts, personalized offers or event registration.

The objective is not to attach a hard-sell link to every entertaining post. That would weaken the content. The objective is to create an architecture through which attention can become identifiable customer value when the consumer chooses to progress.

Adopt a portfolio approach to short-form video

The 85.7% video share of the sample indicates that short-form video is now basic infrastructure, not a differentiator.

A more mature operating model would divide video investment across three jobs:

Portfolio layer Indicative resource share Role
Core / always-on ~70% Value, products, recipes, convenience and proven recurring formats
Experimental ~20% New creators, social trends, interactive formats and new platform behaviors
Breakthrough ~10% Culturally ambitious campaigns, reactive moments and high-risk/high-reward ideas

These percentages are managerial guidance, not empirically derived optimal ratios. Their purpose is to prevent two common problems: spending everything on safe promotional content, or chasing every social trend without building brand memory.

Create a unified digital-content measurement layer

The inability to compare true engagement rates using public information is not merely an academic limitation. It illustrates why retailers themselves should integrate content analytics with CRM and commerce data internally.

A senior-management dashboard should connect:

content ID → audience → RACE objective → media investment → engagement → web/app behavior → loyalty identity → transaction → repeat behavior.

Without that architecture, a 500,000-view video and a 5,000-view video can be incorrectly ranked even where the smaller asset produces substantially more incremental revenue or higher-value customers.

Tesco recommendation in depth

Tesco should resist creating another isolated campaign platform. Its greatest asset is already present: a combination of Clubcard intelligence, broad grocery range, digital fulfillment and nationwide store relevance.

The strategic task is narrative compression.

The brand needs a simple recurring idea capable of making Clubcard, delivery, value, food and community feel like expressions of one customer promise. The communication should answer: What uniquely becomes easier or better in an Irish shopper's life because this is Tesco?

Tesco's owned digital content can then demonstrate the promise through real shopping missions rather than explaining services separately.

A useful management target would be increasing the proportion of customers who use more than one ecosystem feature—for example Clubcard plus online grocery, or Clubcard plus Click & Collect—rather than optimizing each digital service in isolation.

Lidl recommendation in depth

Lidl should protect the creative courage demonstrated by the LGFA campaign. The strategic error would be turning successful cultural properties into routine promotional vehicles.

Instead, Lidl should build a two-speed relationship model.

Purpose, sport and entertainment create Reach and cultural relevance.

Lidl Plus then creates utility and repeat engagement through separate but intelligently connected experiences.

For example, a high-reach LGFA viewer does not need to receive an immediate product discount. The broader audience can later be retargeted with relevant sports-event food, family, community or Lidl Plus utility content, preserving the credibility of the original campaign.

This reflects the logic of integrated communications: integration does not require every message to say the same thing; it requires different touchpoints to contribute coherently to the same relationship. (Kliatchko, 2008).

Aldi recommendation in depth

Aldi should treat "It's Not Complicated" as an operating principle, not merely an advertising line.

Every digital feature should be evaluated against one question:

Does this reduce the perceived complexity of grocery shopping?

Potential applications include simple weekly-shop planning, transparent price communication, uncomplicated meal solutions, predictive stock information for Specialbuys, store navigation and frictionless digital shopping lists.

This enables Aldi to build digital utility without imitating Tesco's loyalty architecture.

The greatest strategic risk would be adding digital mechanics purely because competitors have them. A complicated rewards program would contradict the very positioning that currently differentiates Aldi.

SuperValu recommendation in depth

SuperValu should create a federated digital-content operating system for its local retailer network.

Central teams should provide data, templates, content standards, campaign assets, measurement and brand safeguards.

Local retailers should supply the genuinely differentiating component: people, local products, community knowledge, events and store personality.

A central-only strategy would sacrifice authenticity. A completely decentralized strategy risks inconsistent quality and lost measurement. A federated model preserves both.

TidyTowns should sit at the top of this local ecosystem as an earned-trust property rather than become a direct-sales program. However, audiences engaging with TidyTowns can be invited into broader local SuperValu ecosystems where supplier stories, recipes, events, Real Rewards and store services are discoverable.

This is potentially SuperValu's most defensible response to discount competition. Aldi and Lidl can match individual prices; they cannot quickly reproduce decades of distributed community relationships.

Dunnes Stores recommendation in depth

Dunnes should build Simply Better into a digital content-commerce platform.

The ingredients already exist: recognizable premium private label, Irish suppliers, Neven Maguire, strong food presentation, vouchers and digital fulfillment. Bord Bia's 2026 assessment confirms the strategic relevance of all of these components. (Bord Bia, 2026).

Section 8

Limitations and References

Limitations

The first limitation is sampling completeness. The 49-item dataset is a purposive analytical corpus of publicly discoverable retailer-owned content, not a complete archive of every item published by the five retailers between January and June 2026. Historic social-platform indexing is uneven, and inaccessible or non-indexed stories, short-lived formats and app-only communications are likely underrepresented.

Second, the sample sizes are intentionally modest and almost balanced but not perfectly equal: SuperValu has nine coded units versus ten for each other retailer. Percentages should therefore be interpreted directionally.

Third, SuperValu requires a methodological exception because of its franchise structure. Official TidyTowns properties and two local franchise-page price executions were included. This improves representation of how SuperValu actually communicates locally but reduces perfect organizational equivalence with centrally managed competitors.

Fourth, the study gives strong weight to Facebook/video content because this was the most consistently publicly accessible historic material. Instagram, TikTok, email, push notifications, app personalization and closed loyalty communications cannot be audited to the same degree from the open web. The results therefore describe publicly visible owned digital strategy, not the entirety of each retailer's personalized marketing activity.

Fifth, platform engagement figures are incomplete and not normalized for follower count, reach, paid amplification, campaign duration or algorithmic distribution. A raw view comparison is consequently unsuitable for determining which retailer has the "best engagement." The report intentionally uses median observations and outlier discussion rather than constructing an unjustified league table.

Sixth, promotional intensity is based on the presence of an explicit commercial incentive in a communication. It does not measure the proportion of each retailer's total assortment on promotion, the financial depth of discounting or the incremental effectiveness of those offers.

Seventh, RACE classifications identify the dominant inferred communication objective. Content can perform multiple roles simultaneously. A sports sponsorship can create Reach, Act and long-term Engage effects even where it is coded primarily as Reach.

Eighth, the study does not establish causality between digital content and market share. Lidl's share increased during the period and its communications were strategically strong, but the market outcome is also affected by estate expansion, pricing, assortment, availability, consumer demographics, store experience, media investment and competitors' actions. The market-share figures are contextual benchmarks, not dependent variables in a causal model.

Finally, this is a retailer-side communications analysis rather than consumer perceptual research. The positioning map captures what the brands communicate, not what a statistically representative sample of Irish consumers necessarily believes. A subsequent study could validate these findings using consumer surveys, social listening, loyalty data or experimental creative testing.
